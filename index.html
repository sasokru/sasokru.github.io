A reading list, kept by hand

Digital health,
read closely

A there's a lot happening nowadays (lots of noice also) with AI in medicine. These are the sources I actually read to cut through it, which a mix of reported journalism, clinical evidence, and the responsible-AI research that my own writing draws on. Each one comes with a note on why it earns the attention.

Reported journalism

STAT — Health Tech

statnews.com

If I could only keep one, this is it! A real newsroom covering FDA regulation of AI, devices and clinical deployment with proper sourcing. Their free Health Tech newsletter comes out twice a week.

Digital Health Wire

digitalhealthwire.com

Big recommendation! This fast twice-weekly digest of what actually happened in digital health, like product launches and the occasional industry absurdity. Good for staying current without reading everything.

Healthcare IT News

healthcareitnews.com

The hospital-IT side lives in the systems, procurement, and integration stories that matter if you care about how technology lands on an actual ward rather than in a demo.

Evidence & analysis

Ground Truths — Eric Topol

erictopol.substack.com

Topol reads the actual trials and data and tells you clearly what medical AI can and can't yet do.Consistently the most trustworthy signal I've found.

RAISE Health — Stanford

raise.stanford.edu

A Stanford Medicine and HAI initiative on responsible AI in health. This is the academic basics of exactly the questions my essay is about.

The Medical Futurist

medicalfuturist.com

Bigger-picture, "what does this mean" analysis of where medicine and technology are heading.

Why I keep this list

My essay on medical AI argues that a diagnostic model can only ever generalise what it has seen and that bias enters through the data long before anyone notices it in the clinic. Give it a read :)
